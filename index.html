The two-minute pause

It starts with one interruption.

Text received at 8:14 PM

Your Amazon account was charged $799. Tap now to cancel.

Shield says

Do not tap.

Open the official app or call the number on the card. Do not use the link in the message.

Message Very likely scam

The app

Big enough to use under stress.

The home screen keeps the emergency action first, then moves through plain checks one step at a time.

01

Check the message.

Paste text, upload a screenshot, or add a voicemail transcript.

02

Ask five call questions.

Money, secrecy, codes, remote access, threats. No automatic listening.

03

Slow down payments.

Gift cards, crypto, wires, and instant transfer apps get a stronger warning.

04

Recover without shame.

Call the bank, change passwords, report it, and watch for recovery scams.

Message check Very likely scam

Prize claim asks for credit card details.

What it covers

The scams families are seeing now.

Calls Money, secrecy, verification codes, threats, remote access.
Messages Texts, emails, screenshots, and voicemail transcripts.
Links and QR codes Preview first. Open only when the destination makes sense.
Payments Gift cards, crypto, wires, Zelle, Cash App, Venmo.

Workshops

Not a lecture. A rehearsal.

People practice the exact moments that cause panic: a fake charge, a grandchild voice call, a QR code at the mailbox, a payment request.

  1. 01Pick a trusted contact.
  2. 02Set a family verification phrase.
  3. 03Practice ending a pressure call.
  4. 04Leave with recovery steps.
